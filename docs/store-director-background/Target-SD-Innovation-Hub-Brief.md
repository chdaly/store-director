# Target Store Director — Research Brief for Innovation Hub

*Prepared by Karin Smith · For: Kyle Severson, Sean Bryson, Matt Malstrom, Sean Greenlee*
*Sources: Target Store Director future-state persona materials + Store Director call notes*

**Why you're getting this:** background on how Target Store Directors actually work, what blocks them, and where they're already using Copilot — to ground the innovation hub session next week.

---

## 1. The through-line

> **Give Store Directors their leadership job back — time out of task mode, spent coaching their teams and observing their stores.**

Time saved is the mechanism. Coaching capacity is the outcome. Lead with the outcome.

A useful sub-framing: *move the SD from connecting the dots to making the decisions — from execution back to leadership.*

---

## 2. The persona

### The role
The Store Director personifies the Target experience — empowered to make decisions, drive total store performance, and lead through a guest-first lens. They lead through their frontline team to create an easy, inspiring, and friendly experience, setting clear priorities so teams can do their best work.

### What they own
- Accountability for total store results, with recognition and reward aligned to overall store performance
- Bringing the Target guest experience to life
- Store culture and recognition
- Developing and sustaining a strong leadership pipeline
- Setting clear expectations and priorities
- Store brand image and how the team shows up as brand ambassadors
- Community engagement and connection

### How they run the business
- Builds frontline capability and ownership by aligning guest, team, and financial outcomes through disciplined trade-off decisions
- Builds a culture of friendly, reliable service that elevates the guest experience and strengthens merchandising authority
- Develops teams who are proud brand ambassadors; holds leaders accountable to brand standards and guest experience commitments
- Leverages data and technology to make decisions, prioritize, and lead
- Drives impact as a community ambassador

### The four pillars (Stores Experience Activation Plan)
| Pillar | Definition |
|---|---|
| Guest | The most delightful guest experience in retail — easy, inspiring, friendly |
| Team | High-performing, engaged team, led by purpose, generously serving communities |
| Operations | Consistent operations that power the guest experience |
| Financials | Strong, sustainable P&L fueled by clear financial ownership, data-driven decisions, and operational excellence |

### The five stated pain points
1. **Workload & workforce volatility** — unpredictable workload and workforce dynamics create coverage gaps, pulling SDs into constant rebalancing instead of building a stable, developed team.
2. **Unclear prioritization** — everything feels high-priority, with no clear tradeoff guidance when labor is constrained.
3. **Lack of empowerment to lead** — SDs may not feel equipped or granted enough localized autonomy, so they default to execution over leadership and "lead down a level."
4. **Disconnected tools & communication channels** — flooded with information across multiple tools, with no single view of what matters; they spend time connecting the dots instead of acting.
5. **Constantly shifting focus** — shifting priorities from field leaders create frequent firefighting.

## 3. What we heard from Store Directors

### The headline insight
**The SD's job has become a translation job.** Corporate produces content and initiatives in one shape (workstream-specific, pyramid-specific, calendar-specific). The SD manually converts it into another shape (store-specific, day-specific, person-specific). That translation happens in email, in Excel, and increasingly before the workday starts.

### Theme 1 — The day starts at home, off the clock
SDs begin by reviewing email: daily and weekly metrics, plus leader recaps. The workday effectively starts before they arrive, because there's no way to walk in already oriented. *The pre-work is the workaround.*

### Theme 2 — Corporate content arrives in the wrong shape
Reading is workstream-specific, not store-specific. SDs deconstruct and customize it for their own store. Every SD in the chain independently repeats the same translation — the same work, duplicated across the fleet.

### Theme 3 — Analysis pulls them off the floor
Heavy Excel users. The data exists, but reaching it costs floor presence. Every hour in a spreadsheet is an hour not leading.

### Theme 4 — Comms require triage *and* confirmation
MyDayComms isn't just reading — it's triage plus confirming completion. A closed-loop obligation stacked on top of the information load.

### Theme 5 — Orchestration without coordination
Multiple pyramids push initiatives to stores independently. The SD becomes the integration layer, reconciling dates, trainings, reading, and chasing confirmation from in-store ICs.

> **Proof point: ~12 projects, ~50 dates, different owners across multiple pyramids, separate contacts for each.** No one upstream is deconflicting; the SD absorbs it.

### Theme 6 — Scheduling
Effective scheduling is a significant pain point. UKG implementation is in progress.

### Theme 7 — Where they *want* to spend time
Coaching and leadership development is where SDs feel most like leaders. They want out of task mode — and want to help their teams get out of task mode too.

**Store observations suffer the most** — seeing where improvements can be made is the first thing cut.

> **Compounding effect:** an SD trapped in execution can't coach their leaders out of execution either. The persona's "leading down a level" problem replicates one layer further down. Fixing it at the SD level unlocks the whole leadership pipeline.

### Correlation to test
Stores with low guest metrics also appear to carry operational issues. If observation is what suffers most, that may be the connective tissue — less observation means fewer improvements caught, showing up in both operations and guest scores. **Worth testing, not asserting.** If it holds, it turns "give SDs time back" into a measurable performance argument in Target's own language.

## 4. Copilot adoption — the strongest evidence

### Already using it for
- Documenting best practices
- Leadership development
- Auto-adding dates and action items to the calendar
- Analyzing guest feedback
- Building a team map with responsibilities

**Why this matters:** these SDs aren't a cold audience. They're already users, and they self-selected the highest-value use cases without being told to. Nearly every item maps to the persona's ownership list — leadership pipeline, guest experience, setting clear expectations. *Given a tool that removes friction, SDs spend the time on leadership.* That's the proof point for the entire narrative.

### What they want next
- More detailed review of emails
- Better at adding multiple dates to the calendar
- Using it for comms and tracking
- Prompting help to get the right answers
- Report creation — **one SD built their own dashboard because the existing dashboard wasn't useful**

**Why this matters:** the ask list is almost a one-to-one match with the pain points. They're independently requesting exactly what the persona says is broken. And when a user routes around official tooling to build their own dashboard, that's not a feature request — it's evidence the current system doesn't serve them.

## 5. Pain point → art of the possible

| Where they want to be | What blocks it today | What's possible |
|---|---|---|
| Coaching team leads, building the leadership pipeline | Task mode — execution crowds out leadership, and leads are stuck in it too | Routine reporting, reconciliation, and comms automated; hours returned to coaching |
| Store observations with fresh eyes | First thing cut; heavy Excel analysis pulls them off the floor | Ask a plain-language question, get the answer, stay on the floor |
| Leading with clear priorities | 12 projects, ~50 dates, owners across multiple pyramids, no coordination | One orchestration view — initiatives, dates, owners, confirmations, deconflicted |
| Walking in ready to lead the day | Day starts at home in email — metrics plus leader recaps | Store-specific morning brief, ready on arrival, already triaged |
| Acting on corporate direction | Reading is workstream-specific; every SD localizes it alone | Translation done once, localized to this store, not repeated across the fleet |
| Closing the loop with the team | MyDayComms triage plus completion confirmation | Closed-loop comms handled in the flow of work |
| A stable, well-staffed floor | Scheduling pain; UKG implementation underway | Coverage gaps predicted and pre-solved before the shift breaks down |
| Owning decisions locally | Escalation instead of local ownership | Decision support showing the tradeoff and the guardrails |
| Frontline self-sufficiency | Constant "how do I" questions pull leaders off task | Instant answers on the handheld — returns, policy, process steps |

---

## 6. Engagement themes

1. **Start-of-day orientation** — replace the at-home email dig with a store-specific brief ready on arrival.
2. **Translation, done once** — corporate content localized to *this* store automatically.
3. **Answers without Excel** — ask a question, get the number, stay on the floor.
4. **One orchestration view** — all initiatives, dates, owners, and confirmations in a single deconflicted place.
5. **Closed-loop comms** — triage and completion confirmation handled in the flow.
6. **Coaching capacity** — the outcome all five above are in service of.
