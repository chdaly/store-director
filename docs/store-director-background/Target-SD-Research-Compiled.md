# Target Store Director — Compiled Research & Discovery Notes

*Working reference. Sources: Store Director future-state persona (Target materials) + Store Director call notes.*

---

## 1. The through-line

> **Give Store Directors their leadership job back — time out of task mode, spent coaching their teams and observing their stores.**

Time saved is the mechanism. Coaching capacity is the outcome. Always land on the outcome.

A useful sub-framing: *move the SD from connecting the dots to making the decisions — from execution back to leadership.*

Parallel to the Supply Chain + Site Director story, with one important difference: **for Store Directors the bottleneck is comms and orchestration, not data.** Site Directors drown in reports and reconciliation. SDs drown in messages, reading, and coordinating other people's initiatives.

---

## 2. The role, as described in the persona

### What they own
- Accountability for total store results, with recognition and reward aligned to overall store performance
- Bringing the Target guest experience to life
- Store culture and recognition
- Developing and sustaining a strong leadership pipeline
- Setting clear expectations and priorities
- Store brand image and how the team shows up as brand ambassadors
- Community engagement and connection

### How they run the business
- Builds frontline capability and ownership by aligning guest, team, and financial outcomes through disciplined trade-off decisions
- Builds a culture of friendly, reliable service that elevates the guest experience and strengthens merchandising authority
- Develops teams who are proud brand ambassadors; holds leaders accountable to brand standards and guest experience commitments
- Leverages data and technology to make decisions, prioritize, and lead
- Drives impact as a community ambassador

### How the day starts
Store Directors begin by reviewing email — daily and weekly metrics, plus leader recaps — **from home, before the shift.** The workday effectively starts before they arrive, because there is no way to walk in already oriented.

> **Proof point: ~12 projects, ~50 dates, different owners across multiple pyramids, separate contacts for each.** No one upstream is deconflicting; the SD absorbs it.

### The four pillars (Stores Experience Activation Plan)
| Pillar | Definition |
|---|---|
| Guest | The most delightful guest experience in retail — easy, inspiring, friendly |
| Team | High-performing, engaged team, led by purpose, generously serving communities |
| Operations | Consistent operations that power the guest experience |
| Financials | Strong, sustainable P&L fueled by clear financial ownership, data-driven decisions, and operational excellence |

### The systems they work across
| System | Purpose | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Outlook / email | Metrics, leader recaps, corporate reading | Where the day begins, often from home |
| MyDayComms | Store communications | Requires triage *and* confirmation of completion |
| Excel | Analysis and reporting | Heavy use; pulls them off the floor |
| UKG | Scheduling | Implementation in progress; named pain point |
| Copilot | See section 4 | Already in active use |

## 3. What we heard — themes

### Theme 1 — The job has become a translation job
**This is the headline insight.** Corporate produces content and initiatives in one shape — workstream-specific, pyramid-specific, calendar-specific. The SD manually converts it into another shape: store-specific, day-specific, person-specific.

- **Reading arrives in the wrong shape.** It is workstream-specific, not store-specific, so SDs deconstruct and customize it for their own store.
- **The work is duplicated across the fleet.** Every SD in the chain independently performs the same translation.
- **It happens off the clock.** Increasingly the translation starts at home, before the workday. *The pre-work is the workaround.*

### Theme 2 — Orchestration without coordination
Multiple pyramids push initiatives to stores independently. The SD becomes the integration layer — reconciling dates, trainings, and reading, and chasing confirmation from in-store ICs. Twelve projects and fifty dates is the shape of it, with a different owner and contact behind each.

### Theme 3 — Comms require triage *and* confirmation
MyDayComms is not just reading. It is triage plus confirming completion — a closed-loop obligation stacked on top of the information load, and it compounds across every channel.

### Theme 4 — Analysis pulls them off the floor
Heavy Excel users. The data exists, but reaching it costs floor presence. Every hour in a spreadsheet is an hour not leading.

### Theme 5 — Scheduling
Effective scheduling is a significant pain point. UKG implementation is in progress.

### Theme 6 — Store observations are what suffer
Seeing where improvements can be made is the first thing cut when the day fills up. This is the sharpest casualty: not just lost time, but the feedback loop that finds improvements going dark. Problems stay invisible until they surface in the metrics.

### Theme 7 — Where they want to spend time
**Coaching and leadership development** is where SDs feel most like leaders. They want out of task mode — and want to help their teams get out of task mode too.

> **Compounding effect:** an SD trapped in execution cannot coach their leaders out of execution either. The persona's "leading down a level" problem replicates one layer further down. Fixing it at the SD level unlocks the whole leadership pipeline.

### Persona pain points, for reference
The five stated pain points from the Target materials, which the call notes confirm and sharpen:

1. **Workload & workforce volatility** — coverage gaps pull SDs into constant rebalancing instead of building a stable, developed team.
2. **Unclear prioritization** — everything feels high-priority, with no clear tradeoff guidance when labor is constrained.
3. **Lack of empowerment to lead** — SDs may not feel equipped or granted enough localized autonomy, so they default to execution and "lead down a level."
4. **Disconnected tools & communication channels** — flooded with information across multiple tools, with no single view of what matters.
5. **Constantly shifting focus** — shifting priorities from field leaders create frequent firefighting.

### Correlation to test
Stores with low guest metrics also appear to carry operational issues. If observation is what suffers most, that may be the connective tissue — less observation means fewer improvements caught, showing up in both operations and guest scores. **Worth testing, not asserting.** If it holds, it turns "give SDs time back" into a measurable performance argument in Target's own language.

## 4. Copilot adoption — the strongest evidence

### Already using it for
- Documenting best practices
- Leadership development
- Auto-adding dates and action items to the calendar
- Analyzing guest feedback
- Building a team map with responsibilities

**Why this matters:** these SDs are not a cold audience. They are already users, and they self-selected the highest-value use cases without being told to. Nearly every item maps to the persona's ownership list — leadership pipeline, guest experience, setting clear expectations. *Given a tool that removes friction, SDs spend the time on leadership.* That is the proof point for the entire narrative.

### What they want next
- More detailed review of emails
- Better at adding multiple dates to the calendar
- Using it for comms and tracking
- Prompting help to get the right answers
- Report creation — **one SD built their own dashboard because the existing dashboard was not useful**

**Why this matters:** the ask list is almost a one-to-one match with the pain points. They are independently requesting exactly what the persona says is broken. And when a user routes around official tooling to build their own dashboard, that is not a feature request — it is evidence the current system does not serve them.

## 5. Use cases to land

Tiered by how much has to be true before they work. Tier 1 is already proven in their hands or runs on M365 they already have; Tier 2 needs content or data connection; Tier 3 needs access to Target systems.

### Tier 1 — Land first

**1. Deeper email review and action extraction**
Pull the actions, owners and dates out of the morning email load, and turn metrics and leader recaps into a store-specific read.
- *Evidence:* they asked for "more detailed review of emails" by name; the day already starts in the inbox, from home.
- *Unblocks:* the at-home morning, and the front half of the translation problem.
- *Must be true:* nothing beyond mail — this is why it goes first.

**2. Reliable multi-date calendar capture**
Take a piece of corporate reading with many dates and land all of them, with the action items attached.
- *Evidence:* already using Copilot for auto-adding dates and action items — *and* named "better at adding multiple dates" as the top gap. A working use case with a known ceiling.
- *Unblocks:* the fifty-dates problem, at the individual level.
- *Must be true:* nothing beyond calendar. The fastest credibility win available.

**3. Best practice documentation and leadership development content**
- *Evidence:* both already in active use, unprompted.
- *Unblocks:* directly funds the coaching time they say they want.
- *Must be true:* already true. Use these as the reference story for peers.

**4. Team map with responsibilities**
- *Evidence:* already in use.
- *Unblocks:* clear expectations and pipeline visibility, both on the ownership list.
- *Must be true:* already true.

### Tier 2 — Build toward

**5. Store-specific morning brief**
Walk in already oriented: metrics, recaps, and what matters today, localized to this store and already triaged.
- *Evidence:* the at-home email routine exists precisely because this does not.
- *Unblocks:* the pre-work workaround. **This is the flagship.**
- *Must be true:* access to the metrics and recap sources, and a definition of what "healthy store today" looks like.

**6. Translation done once**
Corporate reading localized to this store automatically, instead of every SD deconstructing it alone.
- *Evidence:* reading is workstream-specific, not store-specific; the same conversion is repeated across the fleet.
- *Unblocks:* the duplicated-work problem — the highest-leverage fix in the whole set, because it scales across every store at once.
- *Must be true:* access to the corporate content pipeline, and store-level context to localize against.

**7. Self-serve reporting and views**
Report creation on their terms, including the dashboard one SD built because the standard one was not useful.
- *Evidence:* named as a want; one SD already routed around the official tooling.
- *Unblocks:* Excel time, and floor presence.
- *Must be true:* access to the underlying store data, not just the existing dashboard layer.

**8. Guest feedback analysis**
Already in use individually; the opportunity is doing it consistently and at scale.
- *Evidence:* on the current-use list.
- *Unblocks:* the guest pillar, and potentially the operations/guest correlation.
- *Must be true:* which feedback sources are reachable.

### Tier 3 — Gated on Target system access

**9. Initiative orchestration view**
All initiatives, dates, owners and confirmations in a single deconflicted place.
- *Evidence:* 12 projects, ~50 dates, multiple pyramids, separate contacts.
- *Unblocks:* the orchestration burden — and, if it works upstream, prevents it rather than absorbing it.
- *Must be true:* whether initiative data exists anywhere centrally, or only in the SD's head. **Answer this before promising it.**

**10. Closed-loop comms**
MyDayComms triage and completion confirmation handled in the flow of work.
- *Evidence:* triage plus confirmation is a named, compounding obligation.
- *Unblocks:* a daily chore that no one has questioned.
- *Must be true:* MyDayComms integration.

**11. Coverage prediction**
Gaps anticipated and rebalancing pre-suggested before the shift breaks down.
- *Evidence:* scheduling named as a major pain point; UKG in progress.
- *Unblocks:* the volatility that pulls SDs off leadership work.
- *Must be true:* UKG connection — the same open question as the Site Director side.

**12. Frontline answers on the handheld**
Any team member gets instant answers — returns, policy, process steps — without pulling a leader off task.
- *Evidence:* the "leading down a level" pattern in the persona.
- *Unblocks:* the interruption tax on leaders.
- *Must be true:* access to policy and process content, plus a handheld surface.

## 6. Store visit observation checklist

**The morning routine** — what they read before arriving, and what they check first once in the building.

**Disconnected tools** — *inventory every distinct device, app, and screen* the SD and team leads touch. Literally count them. Watch for anyone reconciling two screens by hand or re-keying information.

**Prioritization** — how the day's priorities are communicated; whether a single visible "what matters most today" exists; how many task sources compete.

**Workload volatility** — how coverage is displayed (whiteboard, printout, app) and how often it is re-jockeyed; whether the SD is covering a task rather than leading.

**Interruptions** — count redirections during the walk, and what triggers each.

**Empowerment** — which decisions the SD makes on the spot versus escalates (markdowns, staffing, guest recovery).

**By pillar** — *Guest:* in-stock and on-shelf availability, checkout and guest services wait times, live guest recovery, friction points. *Team:* huddles, visible recognition, onboarding of new or seasonal members, shift-to-shift handoffs. *Operations:* backroom and fulfillment flow, task tracking, exception handling, paper versus digital, duplicate entry. *Financials:* which dashboards are actually used, data freshness, one view or many.

**Ownership cross-check** — culture and recognition (visible or ad hoc?), leadership pipeline (developing TLs or filling gaps?), brand image, community engagement.

## 7. How to make it land

Do not lead with capabilities. **Capture one or two vivid moments** where an SD is connecting dots, firefighting, or leading down a level. In the follow-up, play that exact moment back — solved.

Nothing sells meaningful change like their own store's example.

## 8. Comparison to the Supply Chain + Site Director findings

Both audiences want out of assembly work and into leadership. The shape differs:

| | Store Director | Supply Chain + Site Director |
|---|---|---|
| Core burden | Translation and orchestration | Data assembly and reconciliation |
| Where it happens | Email, MyDayComms, Excel | Reports, dashboards, Excel |
| Named systems | MyDayComms, UKG, Excel | Origami, Greenfield/PBI, Workday, Apollo, UKG, Excel |
| Signature proof point | ~12 projects, ~50 dates, multiple owners | 90+ minutes of morning planning |
| Displaced work | Store observations | Talent, culture, upskilling |
| Adoption evidence | Already active Copilot users with self-selected use cases | Asking about scheduled prompts, notebooks, analytics |
| Shared ground | UKG scheduling pain, heavy Excel, morning starts in email, leadership time crowded out by task mode |

## 9. Open questions, by use case

Only what is still unresolved, tied to the use case it gates. **[ASK]** goes to the customer; **[VALIDATE]** is ours to answer internally or with the account team.

### Gates use case 1 — Email review and action extraction
- [ASK] Walk me through your morning before you get to the store — what are you reading, and where?
- [ASK] How much of that reading is genuinely actionable versus informational?
- [VALIDATE] How widespread is the at-home morning routine across the SD population?

### Gates use case 2 — Multi-date calendar capture
- [ASK] Where does it break today — volume of dates, the format they arrive in, or something else?

### Gates use case 5 — Store-specific morning brief
- [ASK] What tells you the store is healthy? Is that one number or several?
- [VALIDATE] Which metric and recap sources are reachable?

### Gates use case 6 — Translation done once
- [ASK] When corporate reading comes through, how much of it applies to your store as written?
- [VALIDATE] Where does corporate content originate, and could localization happen upstream instead of per store?

### Gates use case 7 — Self-serve reporting
- [ASK] What did the dashboard you built do that the standard one did not?
- [VALIDATE] How many SDs have built their own workarounds — dashboards, trackers, spreadsheets?
- [VALIDATE] Is there access to underlying store data, or only to the existing dashboard layer?

### Gates use case 9 — Initiative orchestration
- [ASK] How many active initiatives are you tracking, and how many different people own them?
- [VALIDATE] Is the ~12 projects / ~50 dates figure typical or an outlier store?
- [VALIDATE] Does initiative data exist centrally anywhere, or only with each pyramid owner?

### Gates use case 10 — Closed-loop comms
- [VALIDATE] What does MyDayComms integration require?

### Gates use case 11 — Coverage prediction
- [ASK] How is UKG changing your scheduling day-to-day — better, worse, or just different?
- [VALIDATE] What is the UKG rollout timeline, and who owns it?

### Cross-cutting
- [ASK] If you got two hours back tomorrow, what would you actually do with them?
- [ASK] When was the last time you did a real store walk — not fixing something, just observing?
- [ASK] Are your team leads stuck in task mode too?
- [VALIDATE] Does the low-guest-metrics / operational-issues correlation hold in the data?
