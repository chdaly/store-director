# Target Supply Chain + Site Director — Compiled Research & Discovery Notes

*Working reference. Source: Supply Chain + Site Director discovery call.*
*Target participants (top speakers): Jennifer Rider, Jennifer Hunter, Nicholas Coulter, Nathan Campbell, Benjamin Kremer.*

---

## 1. The through-line

> **Site Directors spend their mornings assembling the health of the building by hand. Give them that picture on arrival, and give the time back to talent and culture.**

The mechanism is data assembly — pulling, cross-checking, and reconciling across systems. The outcome is upskilling and talent development, which is where they say they want to spend their time.

Parallel to the Store Director story, with one important difference: **for Site Directors the bottleneck is data, not comms.** SDs are drowning in messages and orchestration. Site Directors are drowning in reports, dashboards, and reconciliation.

---

## 2. The role, as described on the call

### What they're accountable for
- **Safety — the highest priority**, stated explicitly and unambiguously
- Financials
- Throughput
- Productivity and performance
- Talent, culture, and development

### How the day starts
Recaps arrive by email. They review reports to establish the current **"health of the building."** Calendars are updated in the morning as they determine what to prioritize and who they need to connect with. The plan of day is constructed, not delivered.

> **Proof point: 90 minutes or more every morning** spent planning — pulling data, reviewing it, and prioritizing.

### The systems they work across
| System | Purpose | Note |

## 3. What we heard — themes

### Theme 1 — Email triage: inform versus action
Sorting informational email from email that requires action is a challenge, and it's the gate on building the plan of day. Prioritizing action out of the inbox came up as a distinct theme, separate from the volume problem.

### Theme 2 — "Health of the building" is assembled by hand
The morning report review exists to answer one question: what's the state of this site right now? Today that answer has to be built rather than read, and three things make it expensive:

- **Multiple sources.** Origami, Greenfield, Workday, and Apollo each hold part of the picture, and pulling them together is manual.
- **Conflicting data quality.** Sources disagree, so cross-checking has to be done before the data can be trusted.
- **Reconciliation on top of retrieval.** The cross-check is work layered on the pull — and together they are the clearest driver of the 90-minute morning.

### Theme 3 — Visibility gaps: single-site, single-moment
Dashboards provide a single-site view with **little visibility into multi-site or enterprise views.** Trending over time for a site is also difficult to see — *"good catches"* was the example given. So the picture they can assemble is not only manual, it's narrow: one building, right now.

### Theme 4 — Data is hard to reach, hard to shape, hard to go deep in
Data sources can be large or not specific enough, and data is **layered and hard to find** — which makes checking in and catching blindspots harder than it should be. It's currently difficult to customize views for what they actually need, and they want **more advanced analytics** to dig deeper rather than stopping at what a dashboard shows.

### Theme 5 — Data entry could be streamlined
Not all types are known, but **safety data entry specifically** was called out as a candidate for streamlining.

### Theme 6 — Report once, present many
They often need to take action on data and then present it back in different views to different audiences — PowerPoint and Excel. Reporting splits into views for **"outside the building"** and **"inside the building."** Each audience view is rebuilt by hand today.

### Theme 7 — Weekly staffing optimization
A named challenge with a defined process. To build the weekly plan they look at:
- Site demand
- Labor availability (UKG — new tool)
- Who is volunteering
- Overtime impact
- Special projects and training that may consume resources
- A lookback on performance, attendance, and volunteers

### Theme 8 — Where they want to spend time
**Talent and culture** — upskilling, content, and talent development. The same pattern as Store Directors: the leadership work is what gets displaced by the assembly work.

## 4. What they asked about — stated interest

These came directly from the participants, unprompted:

- **Scheduled prompts** — a Sunday run to make the start of the week easier
- **Notebooks** — explicit interest in understanding how they could help
- **Prompts for consistent reporting and views** — asked as an open question
- **Connecting UKG** — flagged as a new tool, with connection raised as a possibility
- **More advanced analytics** — the ability to dig deeper into the data
- **End-of-shift hand-off and next best action** — described as a potential low-hanging use case, *depending on data availability*

## 5. Use cases to land

Tiered by how much has to be true before they work. Tier 1 runs on content they already have; Tier 2 needs data plumbing; Tier 3 they named themselves but gated on data access.

### Tier 1 — Land first

**1. Morning plan of day from the inbox**
Triage that separates inform from action, then drafts the day's priorities and who they need to connect with.
- *Evidence:* inform-versus-action sorting is the stated gate on the plan of day; calendars get rebuilt every morning off that read.
- *Unblocks:* the front half of the 90 minutes.
- *Must be true:* nothing beyond mail and calendar — this is why it goes first.

**2. Report once, present many**
One set of facts, rendered into inside-the-building and outside-the-building views without rebuilding each by hand.
- *Evidence:* they take action on data then present it back to different audiences in PowerPoint and Excel; they asked directly whether prompts can drive consistent reporting and views.
- *Unblocks:* repeat rebuild work, and the inconsistency that comes with it.
- *Must be true:* a defined set of audience views. Worth capturing two real examples on the next call.

**3. Sunday scheduled prompt**
A start-of-week package that lands before Monday.
- *Evidence:* they raised this specifically, unprompted.
- *Unblocks:* the Monday version of the 90-minute problem.
- *Must be true:* agreement on what belongs in it — best defined once the morning brief exists.

### Tier 2 — Build toward

**4. "Health of the building" on arrival**
A single reconciled morning picture across Origami, Greenfield/PBI, Workday and Apollo — with source disagreements surfaced rather than buried.
- *Evidence:* the 90-minute morning, and cross-checking driven by conflicting data quality.
- *Unblocks:* the core of the daily assembly burden. **This is the flagship.**
- *Must be true:* connectivity to each source, and a decision on which source wins when two disagree.

**5. Beyond the single site, beyond the single moment**
Multi-site and enterprise rollups, plus trending by site.
- *Evidence:* dashboards are single-site only; trending is hard to see, "good catches" the example.
- *Unblocks:* pattern recognition and blindspot detection that isn't possible today.
- *Must be true:* whether any multi-site view exists at any level today.

**6. Depth on demand**
Customizable views and advanced analytics — the notebooks conversation.
- *Evidence:* sources are large or not specific; data is layered and hard to find; they want to dig deeper and asked about notebooks by name.
- *Unblocks:* analysis that currently stops where the dashboard stops.
- *Must be true:* query access to the underlying data, not just the presentation layer.

### Tier 3 — Named by them, gated on data

**7. End-of-shift hand-off and next best action**
- *Evidence:* they called it low-hanging — *and conditioned it on data availability themselves.* That condition is the real qualifier, so it sits here rather than in Tier 1.
- *Unblocks:* continuity between shifts.
- *Must be true:* which shift-level data is actually reachable. Answer this before promising it.

**8. Weekly staffing scenarios**
Demand, UKG availability, volunteers, overtime, special projects and the performance/attendance lookback in one place, with tradeoffs visible.
- *Evidence:* a named challenge with an already-defined six-input process.
- *Unblocks:* a weekly build that is currently manual reconciliation.
- *Must be true:* whether UKG can be connected, and what that requires.

**9. Streamlined safety capture**
Lighter data entry, with safety trends surfaced rather than sought.
- *Evidence:* safety is the stated top priority; Origami entry was called out specifically.
- *Unblocks:* entry time, and trend visibility on the metric that matters most.
- *Must be true:* what safety entry involves today, and what else beyond safety is being keyed in — the participants weren't sure themselves.

## 6. Site visit observation checklist

**The morning routine** — observe the actual sequence: which systems open first, how many logins, where cross-checking happens, and how the plan of day gets written down.

**Data assembly** — count the sources touched to answer one question. Watch for reconciling two screens by hand, re-keying, or rebuilding the same view repeatedly.

**Dashboards in use** — which are actually opened, how current the data is, and what gets exported to Excel immediately after.

**Data entry** — where it happens, how long it takes, and whether the same fact is entered twice. Safety entry specifically.

**Reporting** — what gets rebuilt in PowerPoint or Excel, for whom, and how often.

**Staffing build** — if timing allows, observe the weekly plan being assembled and note each input source.

**Shift hand-off** — what's captured, how, and what's lost between shifts.

**Safety** — how safety data surfaces in the daily rhythm, and whether trending (good catches) is visible or has to be requested.

---

## 7. How to make it land

Same approach that worked for the Store Director story: **capture one vivid moment** — the 90-minute morning, or a cross-check between two systems that disagree — and play that exact moment back, solved.

The 90-minute figure is the strongest single number from this call. It's concrete, it's theirs, and it converts directly into talent-development time.

---

## 8. Comparison to the Store Director findings

Both audiences want out of assembly work and into leadership. The shape differs:

| | Store Director | Supply Chain + Site Director |
|---|---|---|
| Core burden | Translation and orchestration | Data assembly and reconciliation |
| Where it happens | Email, MyDayComms, Excel | Reports, dashboards, Excel |
| Named systems | MyDayComms, UKG, Excel | Origami, Greenfield/PBI, Workday, Apollo, UKG, Excel |
| Signature proof point | ~12 projects, ~50 dates, multiple owners | 90+ minutes of morning planning |
| Displaced work | Store observations | Talent, culture, upskilling |
| Shared ground | UKG scheduling pain, heavy Excel, morning starts in email, leadership time crowded out by task mode |

## 9. Open questions, by use case

Only what's still unresolved, tied to the use case it gates. **[ASK]** goes to the customer; **[VALIDATE]** is ours to answer internally or with the account team.

### Gates use case 1 — Morning plan of day
- [ASK] Walk me through the 90 minutes — what are you pulling, in what order, and where does it take longest?
- [ASK] What tells you the building is healthy? Is that one number or several?
- [VALIDATE] Is 90 minutes typical across sites, or specific to the participants on this call?

### Gates use case 2 — Report once, present many
- [ASK] Where does "outside the building" reporting differ most from "inside the building"?
- [ASK] Can we take two real examples of each to work from?

### Gates use case 4 — Health of the building
- [ASK] When two sources disagree, which one wins, and how do you decide?
- [VALIDATE] Which sources are technically reachable today, and which aren't?
- [VALIDATE] Who owns data quality across Origami, Greenfield, Workday and Apollo? Is anyone reconciling upstream?
- [VALIDATE] What's the Greenfield → Power BI timeline, and who owns it?

### Gates use case 5 — Multi-site and trending
- [VALIDATE] What multi-site or enterprise views exist today at any level — or is the gap total?

### Gates use case 6 — Depth on demand
- [VALIDATE] Is there query access to underlying data, or only to the presentation layer?

### Gates use case 7 — End-of-shift hand-off
- [ASK] What would a good hand-off actually contain?
- [VALIDATE] Which shift-level data is reachable? This is the condition they set themselves.

### Gates use case 8 — Weekly staffing
- [ASK] Which input is hardest to get: demand, availability, volunteers, or the lookback?
- [VALIDATE] Can UKG be connected, and what would that require?

### Gates use case 9 — Safety capture
- [VALIDATE] What data entry exists beyond safety? The participants weren't sure themselves.
